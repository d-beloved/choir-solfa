    How to read the score
    
    
  
    
  
    
    
    Tonic solfa writes each note as a short name instead of a dot on lines. Everything on your sheet is explained here.
    

    
    
      
    1The notes
    
      
    
        dCrDmE
        fFsGlA
        tBd1C
      
    
      
    doh, ray, me, fah, soh, lah, te, doh. The letters show the keyboard notes when doh is C, as an example.
    
      
      
        
    • Doh is home. The top of the score says which note doh is, for example "Doh is B♭". Play that note on a keyboard or a piano app, sing up d r m f s l t d¹, then find your first note.
      • 
        
    • m to f and t to d¹ are close together (a half step). Every other pair of neighbours is a whole step apart.
      • 
        
    • Higher and lower: a small 1 above a note, like d¹ or r¹, means the one above the top doh. A small 1 below, like s₁ or l₁, means the one below doh.
      • 
        
    • Tenor and bass are written an octave higher than they sound, as is usual in solfa. Just sing them in your normal voice.
      • 
        
    • Raised and lowered notes: fe, se and de are a little above f, s and d. ta and ma are a little below t and m.
      • 
        
    • Minor songs: "minor: lah is A" means the tune centres on lah instead of doh. Read the notes the same way.
      • 
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    2The rhythm
    
      
    
        
    |Bar line: a new bar starts.
    
        
    :Beat mark. Each space between marks is one beat (count 1, 2, 3…).
    
        
    ¦The middle of a 4-beat bar, or of a 6/8 bar. Count it like any other beat.
    
        
    -Keep holding the note before it through this beat.
    
        
    (empty)A rest: be silent for that beat.
    
        
    d.rTwo notes in one beat, half a beat each.
    
        
    d,r.m,fFour quick notes in one beat.
    
        
    d.,rLong then short in one beat: three quarters, then a quarter.
    
        
    4 bars restThat part is silent for 4 bars.
    
        
    M. 80The speed: 80 beats a minute.
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    3Try it: Silent Night
    
      
    The tune starts like this, in 3/4 time (three beats in each bar):
    
      
    
        
    s1Si–
    
        
    -.l2 &–i–
    
        
    s3lent
    
        
    m1night
    
        
    -2
    
        
    -3
    
      
    
      
      
        
    • Sing s on beat 1.
      • 
        
    • -.l: keep holding s for the first half of beat 2, then sing l for the second half.
      • 
        
    • s on beat 3. Then the bar line, and m held for all three beats of the next bar.
      • 
        
    • Count steadily, "1 and 2 and 3 and", while you sing.
      • 
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    4Practise your part
    
      
      
        
    • Find your line: S is soprano, A alto, T tenor, B bass. The marks line up down the page, so all four parts move together.
      • 
        
    • Practice audio (MIDI): open it in GarageBand, MuseScore (free) or any music app that plays MIDI. Each part is its own track, named Soprano, Alto, Tenor and Bass. Mute the others to hear just yours, or slow it down.
      • 
        
    • On an instrument: use the letters under the notes in step 1 to play each one.
      • 
        
    • To hand out: the last page of the PDF has a short version of this guide.
